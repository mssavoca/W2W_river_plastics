Model Spec M1 — Methods-Robustness and Source-Attribution Analyses (surface water only)
0. Scope and prime directive
Four additive analyses on the microplastics ERA (probabilistic_risk_characterization.html; edit the .Rmd/.qmd source). Surface water only — river and ocean; do not touch or add sediment. None of these change the production headline (single-slope C-PSD, L_tar_min = 1 µm); they are narrative and clearly-labeled sensitivity/robustness additions. Prose numbers must be inline R references to live objects (no typed literals); every new literature claim carries a citation; seeds are chunk-local; re-render and log each analysis in docs/CHANGELOG_hoffman_revisions.md.
Note on M1.1 vs M1.3 (important — do not conflate): M1.1 is a narrative statement that C-PSD is the preferred, robust method (Segur et al. 2026 already established C-PSD ≈ MLE), and it explicitly does not require fitting MLE to justify the method choice. M1.3 fits MLE (and a tapered/lognormal model) for a different purpose — quantifying model-form/extrapolation uncertainty in the risk result. Keep these separate in the text.

M1.2 — RQ sensitivity to the extrapolation floor L_tar_min (food dilution), with size-consistent tox filtering
Priority: highest — this directly tests whether the sub-floor extrapolation even propagates to the food-dilution conclusion.
Rationale. The production correction factor extrapolates to L_tar_min = 1 µm. Food dilution is volume-weighted, so a power-law explosion of sub-floor particles adds enormous number but little volume; the RQ may be far less sensitive to L_tar_min than the raw number-CF implies. The test is only valid if exposure and hazard use the same lower size bound — so the tox dataset must be filtered to match each floor.
Implementation. For each L_tar_min ∈ {1, 20, 50, 100} µm (river primary; ocean as a secondary panel), holding L_tar_max = 5000 µm:
1.	Exposure: recompute the correction factor over [L_tar_min, 5000] and re-bootstrap the EED (reuse correct_and_bootstrap_eed() with the new L_tar_min).
2.	Hazard — three coordinated changes so the size basis matches (this is the key step):
◦	Set the pSSD++ integration floor to the same bound: x1M_set = x1D_set = L_tar_min in matrix_function() / run_pssd_pipeline() (currently 1).
◦	Filter the ToMEx food-dilution records to exclude studies whose tested particle size is below L_tar_min. Identify and document the ToMEx size field used for the filter (e.g., the reported/aligned particle length used in ERM alignment); define the rule explicitly (exclude a record if its characteristic tested size < L_tar_min; for records spanning the floor, state the rule — e.g., use nominal/mean size, or exclude only if the entire tested range is below the floor — and note it as an assumption). Records with missing size are flagged and excluded from that floor's fit.
◦	Re-derive the food-dilution pSSD++ HC5 from the filtered set at that floor.
3.	Risk: recompute the food-dilution RQ (report both 1-D and MC2D P_exceed_med and RQ_p50) at each floor.
4.	Report a table: L_tar_min | CF_p50 | EED_q50 | n_tox_records | n_species | HC5_median | RQ_p50 (1D, MC2D) | P(RQ>1) (1D, MC2D), and a small figure of RQ vs L_tar_min. Interpret whether the food-dilution exceedance is stable across floors (expected, if volume-weighting damps the sub-floor number explosion) or sensitive.
Guardrails. Production headline stays at L_tar_min = 1. Flag any floor where filtering drops the species count below the pSSD++ minimum (report n_species; treat those floors as indicative only). State clearly that exposure and hazard are size-consistent at every floor (both re-aligned + tox filtered) — this is the point of the analysis.

M1.3 — Model-selection robustness: MLE and a tapered/lognormal small-end model, with GoF and RQ under each
Rationale (distinct from M1.1). Segur's own caveat is that C-PSD extrapolates below the measured range; the marine literature suggests the true small-end may roll off (fewer small particles) rather than continue as a single power law. This analysis quantifies how much the risk result depends on the extrapolation model — it is not a re-justification of C-PSD.
Implementation (river-all and ocean-all length PSDs; by shape optional):
1.	Fit three models to each PSD:
◦	(a) C-PSD OLS (production — carried through for comparison).
◦	(b) MLE power law via poweRlaw::conpl: estimate xmin by minimizing the Kolmogorov–Smirnov statistic (Clauset et al. 2009), estimate α by MLE, and obtain a goodness-of-fit p-value via the bootstrap_p procedure (power law rejected if p < 0.1). 2
◦	(c) Small-end-tapered / lognormal alternative via poweRlaw::conlnorm (and, if feasible, a power-law-with-exponential-cutoff), motivated by marine surface PSDs showing a rapid decrease toward small sizes attributed to selective removal of small particles by sinking/biofouling. 3
2.	Goodness-of-fit / model comparison: report KS D and bootstrap p for the power law, and a Vuong likelihood-ratio test (compare_distributions) for power-law vs lognormal 4; report AIC/BIC where applicable.
3.	Propagate each model to risk: convert each fitted model to the implied number-in-range ratio over [1, 5000] vs the measured LOD window (an equivalent correction factor — for the power law analytic; for lognormal/tapered, the ratio of fitted-CDF mass in the two ranges), then recompute the food-dilution (and tissue-translocation) RQ.
4.	Report a table: Matrix | Model | key params (α or μ,σ) | xmin | KS_D | bootstrap_p | LR vs power law | CF | RQ_p50 | P(RQ>1). Interpret whether the food-dilution exceedance holds across all three models (robustness) or flips under the tapered/lognormal small-end (sensitivity).
Guardrails. C-PSD single-slope remains the production default; MLE/tapered are labeled comparators. Report GoF failures honestly (if the power law is rejected for a matrix, say so). Ocean fiber (5 bins) is excluded from MLE fitting as too sparse. Surface water only.

M1.4 — River vs ocean polymer composition: test for buoyant-polymer enrichment
Rationale. A shift in polymer mix — specifically enrichment of buoyant polymers (PP, PE) at the sea surface relative to rivers — would independently support a surface-retention/transport interpretation of the shallower ocean PSD slope, rather than a source/residence or fitting-window explanation.
Implementation.
1.	Build a river-vs-ocean contingency table of polymer_density_group counts and proportions (river n ≈ 1,207; ocean n = 117). For reference, the river assemblage is polyester/PET 300, polypropylene 212 (buoyant), polyamide/acrylamide 205, styrenics 164, polyethylene 114 (buoyant), and a long tail 5; compute the ocean equivalents.
2.	Test the difference: a chi-square test (or Fisher's exact if sparse cells) on the full polymer × matrix table, and a focused 2×2 test on buoyant (PP + PE) vs non-buoyant, river vs ocean, reporting each matrix's buoyant fraction with binomial 95% CIs.
3.	Assign buoyancy to the correct medium: the data already carry buoyancy_freshwater (vs 1.00 g cm⁻³); add buoyancy_marine for ocean particles relative to seawater (1.025 g cm⁻³, already the water density used for ocean). Note that PP (0.90) and PE (0.94) are buoyant in both media, while borderline classes (styrenics/silicone ≈ 1.05) flip near neutral in seawater — handle explicitly.
4.	Visualize: grouped/stacked bar of polymer composition by matrix, and a labeled buoyant-fraction comparison.
5.	Interpret: significant PP/PE enrichment in ocean surface water supports the transport/retention reading of the slope difference; no significant shift points to source/residence or a fitting-window artifact.
Caveats to state. Composition is count-based over the measured window (same LOD/window caveats as the slopes); ocean n = 117 limits power. Add a one-line companion note that the river–ocean slope difference itself is partly confounded by different fitting windows (river 165–385 µm vs ocean 95–245 µm) and should be re-tested over a common overlapping window before the slope difference is attributed to source/transport — the polymer-composition test in this section is the independent line of evidence that does not depend on the fitting window.
