1. Why reframe
The M2 decomposition is correct, but §12.5.1.3 currently leads with a three-arm structure in which Arm A is not a physically valid risk quotient — it holds the HC5 pinned at the 10 µm alignment while sliding the exposure floor to 1 µm, i.e. it quotes exposure over [1, 5000] against hazard over [10, 5000]. The document already acknowledges this: Arm A deliberately mismatches the exposure and hazard size bases – every Arm A/B number above is a diagnostic, not a valid stand-alone risk quotient. 1
A reader correctly objects that exposure and hazard must always be aligned to the identical size range. Two of the three arms satisfy that rule and are physically interpretable:
•	Arm B — composition frozen (tox_fixed) but the hazard-integration bound now tracks L_tar_min too 1, so exposure and hazard are both aligned to [L_tar_min, 5000]; the only thing held fixed is which studies are SSD members.
•	M12equiv — composition ALSO tracks L_tar_min (filter_tox_by_size_floor re-applied at every floor), i.e. exactly M1.2's own pipeline 1.
Their difference isolates the study-inclusion (composition) effect cleanly and without any size-basis mismatch, because both arms rescale exposure and hazard coherently to the same range. Arm A is only needed as a further sub-split of Arm B's coherent trajectory into "exposure de-extrapolation vs alignment-range shift," and its absolute values are non-physical by construction — it enters the decomposition only as a canceling difference (see §4).
Reframe objective: make Arm B → M12equiv the primary narrative; demote Arm A to a clearly-labeled supplementary sub-decomposition.

2. The scientific conclusion this section must land (exact wording)
State it in these terms — the threshold-crossing framing, with both honesty caveats — and do not overstate:
With both exposure and hazard rescaled coherently to a common [L_tar_min, 5000] µm range but the toxicity study set held fixed (Arm B), the river Food Dilution RQ at a 1 µm exposure floor is 0.636 (1D) — below 1. Allowing the study set to open as the floor drops (M12/production) collapses the Food Dilution HC5 from ~1200 to ~68 and raises the RQ to 9.4. The 1 µm-floor exceedance is therefore created by admitting <10 µm-tested studies into the food-dilution SSD (a hazard/HC5 effect), not by coherent sub-10 µm extrapolation.
Mandatory caveats in the same passage (single source of truth; pull numbers via inline R, do not retype):
1.	Magnitude vs threshold-crossing. Sub-10 µm exposure de-extrapolation is not negligible — it is the larger single lever by magnitude (river Food Dilution exposure-CF fold 80.51× vs composition fold 14.78× 1). It fails to cross 1 only because it operates on a fixed ≥10 µm study set with a very low baseline RQ. Phrase as "study inclusion is decisive for threshold-crossing," never "extrapolation is irrelevant."
2.	Metric dependence. The clean "<1 without study inclusion" statement is strictly true in 1D for the river; under MC2D the river Arm B Food Dilution RQ is 1.508 versus M12's 12.383 1. Ocean Arm B stays ≤1 under both metrics. Report both; state that the study-inclusion gap (~8–15×) and the qualitative conclusion hold under both metrics.

4. One-line mathematical note to add (resolves the "mismatch doesn't make sense" objection)
Add this after the Arm-A relegation note, because it is the crux of why the size-mismatch is harmless:
Because the exposure-CF term is a difference of two Arm-A quantities that share the identical pinned HC5, the HC5 divides out: ΔlogRQ_exposureCF(f) = log₁₀[EED(f)/EED(10)]. This is verifiable against the raw exposure: river Food Dilution EED q50 is 646.428 at 1 µm and 7.499 at 10 µm 1 → ratio ≈ 86×, matching the reported exposure-CF fold of 80.5× within Monte Carlo noise (dlogRQ_exposureCF = 1.906 at the 1 µm river Food Dilution cell 1). Arm A's non-physical absolute value never survives the subtraction.

5.1 Data assembly (single source of truth — reuse existing objects, do not recompute risk)
## Build a two-scenario long df from ALREADY-LIVE objects.
## "Study set fixed (>=10 um)"  = Arm B rows of m2_results (arm == "B")

##                                 per the spec's do-not-re-run-M1.2 rule; for
##                                 Tissue Translocation use m2_results arm == "M12equiv".
## Exposure draws are identical between Arm B and M12equiv at each floor
## (correct_and_bootstrap_eed() depends only on compartment + L_tar_min, not on arm),
## which is what makes the paired panel in Section 5.4 valid.
scenario_levels <- c("Study set fixed (\u226510 \u00b5m tox)",
                     "Study set tracks floor (production)")
m2_scenario_df <- dplyr::bind_rows(
  m2_results |> dplyr::filter(arm == "B") |>
    dplyr::transmute(compartment, ERM, L_tar_min,
                     RQ_p50 = RQ_p50_1D, RQ_lo = RQ_p025_1D, RQ_hi = RQ_p975_1D,
                     scenario = scenario_levels⟨1⟩),
  ## Food Dilution -> m12_results; Tissue Translocation -> m2 M12equiv arm

) |>
  dplyr::mutate(scenario = factor(scenario, levels = scenario_levels))

5.4 Companion panel — m2_studyinc_effect (recommended: honest "effect with uncertainty")
The marginal 1 µm CIs are very wide, so the two scenario error bars may overlap even though medians differ ~15×. Because Arm B and M12equiv use the identical exposure draws at each floor, form the paired per-draw ratio, which cancels shared exposure variance exactly:
RQ_M12,i / RQ_ArmB,i = (EED_i / HC5_M12,i) / (EED_i / HC5_ArmB,i) = HC5_ArmB,i / HC5_M12,i
i.e. a pure hazard-side study-inclusion effect with the dominant exposure noise removed. Plot its median + 95% CI by floor and compartment.
## Requires matched (identically-seeded) draw vectors for Arm B and M12 at each
## (compartment, floor). If m2_results stores only summaries, add a thin harness
## return of the paired RQ draw vectors (hazard side only) -- no new risk logic,
## just retention of the per-draw ratio. EED cancels, so this is exactly HC5_B/HC5_M12.
m2_studyinc_effect <- paired_ratio_df |>
  ggplot2::ggplot(ggplot2::aes(x = factor(L_tar_min), y = ratio_p50)) +
  ggplot2::geom_hline(yintercept = 1, linetype = "dashed", color = "gray40") +
  ggplot2::geom_pointrange(ggplot2::aes(ymin = ratio_p025, ymax = ratio_p975)) +
  ggplot2::facet_wrap(~ compartment) +
  ggplot2::scale_y_log10() +
  ggplot2::labs(x = expression(L[tar-min]~"(\u00b5m)"),
                y = "Study-inclusion effect  RQ(tracks)/RQ(fixed)  = HC5(fixed)/HC5(tracks)  [paired, log10]",
                title = "Study-inclusion effect on RQ, exposure variance removed by pairing")
Label clearly: this is the isolated hazard-side effect; the paired CI is tight precisely because exposure cancels.

6. Self-contained caption (dynamic; glue-populated, no retyped literals)
Figure. The Food Dilution risk exceedance is created by toxicity-study inclusion, not by sub-10 µm size extrapolation. Median Food Dilution risk quotient (RQ, 1D) versus the lower size floor L_tar_min, faceted by compartment; points = median, bars = 95% CI (2.5th–97.5th percentile of the 1D risk draws); dashed line RQ = 1. Blue ("study set fixed ≥10 µm") rescales exposure and hazard coherently to [L_tar_min, 5000] µm but holds the SSD membership fixed; orange ("study set tracks floor") is the production pipeline, which additionally admits studies tested below the floor. Both scenarios rise as the floor decreases — that shared rise is coherent size extrapolation, present in both. The effect of interest is the vertical gap between the curves: at a 1 µm floor the river RQ is {RQ_ArmB_river_1} with the study set fixed but {RQ_M12_river_1} when it tracks the floor (a {fold}× study-inclusion effect), because the food-dilution HC5 collapses from {HC5_ArmB_river_1} to {HC5_M12_river_1} µm-basis when <10 µm-tested studies enter the SSD. Coherent extrapolation alone leaves the river RQ below 1 (1D); under MC2D the fixed-set river RQ is {RQ_ArmB_river_1_mc2d}, still ~{gap_mc2d}× below the tracking scenario. Sub-10 µm exposure de-extrapolation is the larger lever on RQ magnitude ({exposureCF_fold}× vs {composition_fold}× for the river collapse) but is insufficient on its own to cross RQ = 1 from the fixed-set baseline; study inclusion is decisive for the threshold crossing. Diagnostic of model behavior — the production risk characterization is unchanged.
